The COP curve, plain English

A cold-climate ASHP has Coefficient of Performance ~3.0 at 47°F, dropping to ~2.0 at 17°F, dropping to ~1.5 at 5°F. At COP 1.0 the heat pump becomes a resistance heater. NEEP's cold-climate spec list is the canonical reference. NEEP

Annual cost, 1,800 gal-equivalent (212 MMBtu useful) household

  • Oil only: 1,800 gal x $5.54/gal = $9972/yr.
  • ccASHP only: 212 MMBtu x $19.89/MMBtu = $4215/yr at the US-average $0.19/kWh. At New England's ~$0.28/kWh (EIA 2026) it is ~$6211/yr.
  • Dual-fuel (ASHP above 25°F, oil below): ~$5078/yr at the US-average rate.

Even at New England's much higher electricity rate, a ccASHP undercuts oil-only heat while oil sits near $5.54/gal. The margin narrows on the coldest single-digit weeks, when COP falls and dual-fuel earns its keep.

Rebates in 2026 (the 25C credit has expired)

The federal 25C tax credit (30% of cost, up to $2,000) expired for installs after 31 December 2025 under the 2025 budget law. A 2026 install does not qualify. What is left is the income-qualified federal HEAR rebate plus state programmes, which stack. State amounts change yearly and scale with income, so confirm the current figure with the programme before you budget:

  • Federal HEAR (income-qualified): up to $8,000 toward a heat pump for households at or below 150% of area median income.
  • Mass Save (MA): whole-home ASHP rebate is capped (about $8,500 in 2026), rising to $16,000 for income-qualified households.
  • NY State Clean Heat: administered through your electric utility on a per-kBtuh basis, up to about $12,000 depending on system size and utility.
  • Efficiency Vermont: roughly a few hundred to ~$2,200 per unit at standard income, about $3,000 per unit income-eligible, plus utility bonuses.
  • Efficiency Maine: $1,000 per unit at any income (up to $3,000 for three units), rising to $9,000 for income-qualified households.
